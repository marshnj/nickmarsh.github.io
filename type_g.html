G-type Main-Sequence Star Characteristics


G-type stars are yellow to the human eye. G-type stars are also known as yellow dwarf stars. They fuse hydrogen into helium for 10 billion years or so, until they run out of hydrogen in their cores. Once hydrogen is exhausted, they move off of the main sequence, expand, and become red giant stars. In another billion years, the helium core fuzes and the star ejects material and forms a planetary nebula. It then collaspes into a white dwarf star, which slowly cools. Sol is about 4.6 billion years old and Rigel Kentaurus is about 4.8 billion years old. They will gradually heat up and move off of the main sequence in another billion years.

Sol (the Sun)


Our home star, the only star known to have an inhabited planet. There are 8 known planets, five dwarf planets, hundreds of moons, and thousands of asteroids and comets.


Rigil Kentaurus


A member of the three star Alpha Centauri system with Toliman and Proxima Centauri. There are no confirmed planets.
