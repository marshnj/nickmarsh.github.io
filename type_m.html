M-type Star characteristics:


M-type stars, also known as red dwarfs, are cool, low-mass stars with surface temperatures below 3,700 Kelvin. They are characterized by strong molecular absorption bands in their spectra, particularly due to titanium oxide (TiO). M stars have relatively long lifespans, ranging from 1 to 10 trillion years, but their low luminosity means that habitable zones around them are very close to the star, potentially leading to tidally locked planets.


M-type Stars Within 8 Light Years


Proxima Centauri


Proxima Centauri is the closest known star to the Sun, a small red dwarf located just over 4 light-years away in the Alpha Centauri system.


Barnard's Star


Barnard’s Star is a dim red dwarf about 6 light-years away and is the second-closest star system to Earth after the Alpha Centauri trio.


Wolf 359


Wolf 359 is a faint red dwarf located about 7.9 light-years from Earth, known as one of the closest and least luminous stars in our galactic neighborhood.
