# nickmarsh.github.io
Introduction to Web Development Group 2 Final Project

This website was created by Group 2 for CIS-207 Introduction to Web Development at Calhoun Community College. The Group 2 members are Elizabeth Audenaert, Lucas Beasley, and Nick Marsh. The purpose of this website is to display a  3D image map of the six stars within eight light years of the sun, explain what stellar type means, provide the stellar type of each star, and provide interesting facts about these stars. This website targets people who are interested in astronomy, space science, and science fiction. In the case of science fiction, for instance, Wolf 359 is the star where the battle between the Borg and the Federation occurred in the Star Trek: The Next Generation television series. Hundreds of science fiction stories are set at one of the nearby stars.

Group 2 chose eight light years to limit the scope of the website. There are only six stars of three stellar types, including the sun (also referred to as Sol), within eight light years. This required an eleven-page website. If we used ten light years, the number of stars is eleven with four stellar types (A, G, K, and M) and sixteen pages are required. At twelve light years, the number of stars increases to twenty-nine and the number of stellar types is five (A, F, G, K, and M) and thirty-six pages are needed.
 
Each page begins with a heading describing the contents of the page. The title of the home page is “Stars Within Eight Light Years of Earth.”  The home page introduces the two-level navigation bar which appears on each page. The top-level navigation bar  links to the Home page, a separate page on the nature of G type stars, K type stars, and M type stars, and the Newsletter request form. The current page is highlighted in green with yellow text on the navigation bar. The second level of the bar under G, K, and M, lists links to the Stars of that type. There are two G type stars: our Sun (Sol) and Rigil Kentaurus. There is one K type star: Toliman. There are three M type stars: Proxima, Barnard’s Star, and Wolf 359. There is a brief description of what the navigation bar does that only appears on the home page.

The next feature of the home page is a 3D map of the stars within eight light years. Sol is at the center of the map. The lines indicate whether a star is north or south of Sol. This is galactic north and south. The length of the lines and their angle represents the distance and orientation of each star to Sol. The graphic is a map graphic and clicking on the star, or star name, will take you directly to the page for that star. Rigil Kentaurus, Proxima Centauri, and Toliman are part of the three-star Alpha Centauri system, so they are on top of each other. Clicking the name is the best way to make sure you go to the correct star page.

The rest of the page covers the topic of Star Classification. Stars are classified based on temperature, from the hottest (O) to the coolest (M). The table shows the seven stellar types and their temperatures and color. Their mass and radius are shown relative to the size of Sol and also the percentage the type of star is of all stars in the galaxy. The table is a roll-over table. Roll over the table to see a graphic on relative sizes, temperatures, and colors of the seven stellar types. Note that the color difference is exaggerated in the table. To the human eye, stars are so bright that from a distance from each type of star so it appeared the same size as Sol does from Earth, they would all look close to the same color as they appear on the rollover graphic.

The three stellar type pages (G, K, M) begin with a descriptive heading “… Type Stars Within 8 Light Years”, the navigation bar, and a rollover graphic of a table listing the characteristics of the ten sub-types into which each stellar type is divided. There is a brief description that indicates that the image is a rollover and what the two graphics show. The second graphic shows an image of a typical star of that type. The characteristics of the stellar type are followed by a list of the nearby stars of this stellar type with a short description. Each star name also links to the page for that star.

The six pages for the stars (Sol, Rigil Kentaurus, Toliman, Proxima, Barnard’s Star, and Wolf 359) all begin with a descriptive heading and the navigation bar. All the pages, except the page for Sol, have a rollover graphic followed by information specific to that star. The purpose of the rollover graphics differs. Some display a simulated image of the star, relative sizes of stars, constellations, or planetary systems. The page for Sol has a JavaScript button for each planet in the solar system that displays the planet. There is also a button for returning to the original graphic of Sol and one to display the relative sizes of Sol and the planets. These buttons appear below the image.
The last page is the Newsletter page. This page asks visitors interested in getting a newsletter about the latest scientific findings about nearby stars to enter their first and last names and their email addresses. The page contains the two-level navigation bar.
